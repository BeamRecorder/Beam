# Design truth

A complete native Studio workspace, filled by a real Quiet Aurora recording,
becomes a finished composition through a small sequence of precise edits.

1600 × 1000 output; a 1280 × 800 design stage. A 1232 × 752 editor card sits
24 points from each edge. Its native topbar is 42 points high, the inspector
occupies the left side, a large 16:9 preview the right, and four native painted
timeline tracks run across the bottom. Caption, webcam, screen and zooms remain
clearly separated. The timeline gets the first focused camera; properties get
the second; the final wider view shows the edited result. The camera never
chases individual controls: focus views stay fixed at 1.1× and 1.22×, with
0.85–0.95 second transitions and a 5.75 second properties hold.

Use only Beam palette/surface tokens and Hanken Grotesk. Native cursor roles
switch between arrow and horizontal trim. Keep the outside Tahoe wallpaper
proportional. The source canvas begins on Ventura and ends with a native Ocean
gradient. Shadow color becomes violet; recording size gains balanced padding.

Motion follows viewport-change and discrete-content sequencing rules: one
paused GSAP clock, pure time-dependent state, cursor press before the change,
direct trim drags, eased travel, readable holds, and a short fade hiding the reset.
